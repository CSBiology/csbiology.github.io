
Understanding biology begins with finding the right computational representation.

-> We develop computational representations that reveal how biological systems function, adapt, and respond to their environment

Our research develops computational representations that preserve biological meaning, enabling computational reasoning about living systems.

### Research philosophy

Biological systems are extraordinarily complex. They cannot be understood simply by applying the latest computational method. Instead, every biological question requires identifying the computational representation that preserves the essential biological principles. Our research therefore begins with biology rather than algorithms. We develop computational abstractions that faithfully capture biological regulation, biological state, and biological knowledge, enabling new insights into how living systems function and adapt.

# Methods

To address these questions, we combine machine learning, network biology, mathematical modelling, statistical inference, software engineering and FAIR research infrastructures. Rather than favouring a particular methodology, we select or develop computational approaches according to the biological question at hand.



# Why does this matter?


Our goal is not to develop computational methods for their own sake. Our goal is to enable biological discovery. By transforming complex biological observations into interpretable computational representations, we support research in plant acclimation, biotechnology, systems biology and precision medicine, while providing computational foundations that are transferable across biological disciplines.



### Our scientific principles

1. **Biology comes first.** Every computational approach begins with a biological question.
2. **Representation determines discovery.** Biological mechanisms can only be inferred if the computational representation preserves the relevant biological principles.
3. **Methods are tools, not goals.** We combine or develop statistics, machine learning, network models and software according to the needs of the biology.
4. **Understanding is the objective.** Prediction is valuable only when it contributes to explaining how biological systems function.
